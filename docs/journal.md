# Build Journal (`agent-trace-replay`)

One paragraph per build session documenting what we tried, what broke, and what we measured.

## Session 1 (2026-10-18): Keyed SHA-256 Randomness and the Order-Desk World (M1)

We started by replacing Python's stateful `random.Random` with a stateless `draw(seed, *key)` helper backed by SHA-256 top-53-bit extraction (`src/agent_trace_replay/rng.py`). Early in testing `test_rng_and_world.py`, we noticed that naively joining key parts with `str(k)` caused `draw(7, True)` and `draw(7, "True")`—or `draw(7, 1)` and `draw(7, "1")`—to hash to the exact same digest. Prefixing each key component with its canonical type tag (`bool:true`, `int:1`, `float:...`, `str:...`) eliminated cross-type collisions completely. On top of `draw`, we built `OrderDeskWorld` (`src/agent_trace_replay/world.py`) with 12 industrial SKUs, epoch-based price drift (`price_at_epoch(item, E)` as a pure function of `(seed, item, E)`), and an append-only `effect_log` that tags every `reserve_stock`, `create_ticket`, `send_notice`, and `kv_put` write with its `attempt_tag`.

## Session 2 (2026-10-25): Tuning the 2,000-Run Cohort Failure Mix (M2)

We wired the 8 tools (`calc`, `lookup_order`, `get_price`, `get_stock`, `reserve_stock`, `create_ticket`, `send_notice`, `kv_put`) into `ToolRegistry` and wrote the hand-rolled order-desk graph (`plan -> lookup -> price -> [escalate] -> stock -> [backorder] -> reserve -> ticket -> notify -> finish`, plus `give_up`). On our first 2,000-seed sweep with `deadline_ms=250.0` and `flaky_rate=0.14`, the failure rate was way too high: 1,270 out of 2,000 runs (63.50%) failed, dominated by 875 deadline timeouts because retry backoffs (`28ms`, `56ms`) stacked on top of 85ms service hiccups. We swept `deadline_ms`, `hiccup_prob`, `flaky_rate`, and order budget/stock margins until the cohort landed at 466 failed runs out of 2,000 (`23.30%` failure rate, right inside the 15–25% target window): 236 `deadline_exceeded`, 131 `budget_escalated`, 75 `stock_backordered`, and 24 `retry_exhausted`.

## Session 3 (2026-11-01): Trace Projection and the Step-0 Volatile Fingerprint Bug (M3)

We implemented the four capture levels (`L0` transcript, `L1` tool results, `L2` ambient service timings and tie-break draws, `L3` per-step state fingerprints) in `src/agent_trace_replay/trace.py` so a single recorded `RunOutcome` projects down to any level without re-running the agent. When we ran our first L3 replay check, every single run failed at Step 0 (`plan`) with a fingerprint mismatch! Inspecting the state diff showed why: `step_wall_timestamp_ms` and `attempt_tag` (`"replay_0"` vs `"incident_0"`) were stored inside the state dictionary. Excluding `VOLATILE_STATE_KEYS` (`last_call_latency_ms`, `step_wall_timestamp_ms`, `attempt_tag`) before canonical JSON serialization fixed L3 replay immediately. We kept both hashes on `StepRecord` so Experiment 3 could measure the exact failure rate when volatile fields are left in (`2,000/2,000 = 100.00%` Step-0 divergence) and pinned the story in `test_fingerprint_excludes_volatile_fields_and_diverges_when_volatile_included`.

## Session 4 (2026-11-08): Replay Attribution Precedence and Pre-Failure Checkpoints (M4 & M5)

While building `ReplayDriver` (`src/agent_trace_replay/replay.py`) and running Experiment 3 at `L1`, we initially saw 82 of the 466 failed runs report `code_change` at Step 6 (`notify`) with `"Unexpected stochastic choice 'notice_template'"`. Tracing seed 2 revealed that the code had not changed at all: at `L1`, service timings are not captured, so the replay drew faster service times, survived the deadline guard that killed the original run before `notify`, and reached `notify` for the first time. Updating `pick_stochastic_choice` and `before_step` to attribute control-flow divergence to the earliest pending ambient mismatch (`self._pending_cause` from `CLOCK` or `RNG`) cleanly separated `clock` (154 runs) and `rng` (312 runs) at `L1`. In Experiment 2, we also refined `_select_pre_failure_snapshot` so `budget_escalated` and `stock_backordered` resume from the `price` and `stock` decision snapshots rather than after the failure branch was already locked in—revealing 14 budget-escalated incidents that quietly flip into phantom successes when downward price drift occurs between the incident and the debug session.

## Session 5 (2026-11-15): Localization Mutations, Analytic Survival DP, LangGraph Gateway, and Number Guards (M6)

In our final session, we added the four engine mutations (`dropped_state_field`, `skipped_backoff`, `fudged_budget_threshold`, `extra_retry`) for Experiment 4, the keyed survival probability model with a 5ms-grid deadline dynamic program (`src/agent_trace_replay/analytic.py`) for Experiment 5, and the `StateGraph` + `InMemorySaver` bridge (`src/agent_trace_replay/langgraph_bridge.py`) for Experiment 6. Initially, our trajectory comparator in Experiment 5 checked `final_fingerprint` equality (which requires every continuous `service_ms` jitter draw to match to 3 decimal places, giving `0/466` for live reruns) while our analytic model predicted discrete trajectory survival (`5.08%` at `E+0` decaying to `1.99%` at `E+3`). Aligning the trajectory comparator to check discrete trajectory equivalence (node path, attempt counts, selected doc, unit price, and policy choices) brought measured live rerun reproduction (`5.79%` at `E+0`, `1.93%` at `E+3`) into tight agreement with the analytic model (`5.08%` at `E+0`, `1.99%` at `E+3`), while demonstrating why the naive marginal-hazard model underpredicts survival by more than 3x (`0.55%` at `E+3`). Finally, we wired `tools/collect_metrics.py` and `tools/check_numbers.py` into `make verify` so any drift between code outputs and documentation fails the build.
